Method ImplementationFu et al. (CVPR 2016) Weighted Variational Retinex: Separate Channel-wise Decomposition

1. Theory & Mathematical Formulation

Observed physical image S = R · L (Reflectance R, Illumination L). In conventional log-domain s = r + l, the spatial gradient is: ∇(log x) = 1x ∇x.

When x is small (dark regions), the factor 1x explodes and over-penalizes gradients, burying subtle component edges in bright regions. Fu et al. eliminate this with a weighted objective function:

Weighted Variational Energy Functional
minr, l   ‖r + l − s‖22  +  c1 ‖er · ∇r‖1  +  c2 ‖el · ∇l‖22
Constraints: r ≤ 0  and  s ≤ l   |   Weights er = R and el = L directly cancel the 1R and 1L gradient distortion!

Solved via ADMM in 3 sub-problems: (P1) L1 shrinkage soft-thresholding, (P2) 2D FFT closed-form solver for reflectance r, (P3) 2D FFT closed-form solver for illumination l.

2. Our Implementation: Separate RGB Channel Decomposition

Rather than standard grayscale/HSV V-channel processing (which discards color albedo), we implemented Fu et al. in PyTorch with independent 3-channel execution:

🔴 Red: SR → (RR, LR) 🟢 Green: SG → (RG, LG) 🔵 Blue: SB → (RB, LB)
Channel-wise Residual Albedo Fusion
Ifused, c  =  (1 − α) · Iorig, c  +  α · Rrefl, c    (α = 0.30)
Preserves natural PCB copper/substrate colors while extracting shadow-invariant surface albedo across Red, Green, and Blue planes.

Fully vectorized GPU/CPU execution using PyTorch 2D Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT).

Training Result & Physical Finding on YOLO26s (1280px)

Trained on full 4-class PCB dataset: mAP50 = 0.5591 · mAP50-95 = 0.4076 · Recall = 0.5716 · Capacitor AP50 = 0.2673

Physical Insight: Industrial PCB inspection boards are captured under diffuse, flat studio illumination with minimal cast shadows. Removing illumination gradients provided no contrast gain because the primary challenge is microscopic component scale, not non-uniform lighting.

Data Audit & RectificationRecovering Omitted Components: Confidence & Filter Thresholds

Candidate Verification: Visual Failure Case on ATTIOT Board

The Problem with Correlation Alone: Candidates M1–M4 have high cross-correlation (NCC 0.69–0.73) with capacitor templates, but microscopic zoom reveals they are connector pin edges and silkscreen text. Loose thresholds inject severe label noise!

1. The Ground Truth Omission Problem

Annotators omitted hundreds of physical SMD capacitors on genuine boards. When the detector fires on an unannotated capacitor, loss penalizes it as a False Positive: the model is actively trained to suppress small capacitors!

2. The Dual-Verification Pipeline & Strict Thresholds

To recover real components without hallucinating noise, we engineered a dual neural + optical filter:

  • YOLO26x multi-scale proposals @ 1280px native inference
  • Capacitors: Confidence ≥ 0.65 AND NCC ≥ 0.50 AND Aspect Ratio ≥ 1.30
    (Aspect ratio kills circular drill vias; NCC confirms solder fillet reflectance)
  • ICs: Confidence ≥ 0.70 on distinct silicon packaging

3. Impact: +1,175 Verified Labels Added (4,752 → 5,927)

YOLO26x Cleaned vs Original: Capacitor AP50 rose from 0.3077 → 0.3354 (+9.0% relative), and Recall rose from 60.84% → 61.79%.
